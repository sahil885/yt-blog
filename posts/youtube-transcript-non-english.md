## Translating a Non-English Transcript to English

Once you have the transcript in the original language, translating it is straightforward using free AI tools.

**Method 1: Use ChatGPT or Claude**

Paste the foreign language transcript with this prompt:

```
Here is a transcript in [LANGUAGE]. Please translate it to English, maintaining the meaning and tone as closely as possible. Preserve paragraph breaks and structure.

[PASTE TRANSCRIPT]
```

ChatGPT and Claude handle major world languages very well. For less common languages, quality may vary.

**Method 2: Use Google Translate**

For a quick translation without opening a separate AI tool: copy the transcript, go to translate.google.com, paste it into the source field, and set the target language to English. For transcripts under 5,000 characters this works well. Longer transcripts may need to be split.

**Method 3: Use DeepL**

DeepL (deepl.com) is widely considered the most accurate machine translation tool for European languages. If you're translating Spanish, French, German, Italian, or Portuguese content, DeepL often produces more natural results than Google Translate.

## What Languages Does YouTube Auto-Caption Support?

YouTube's automatic speech recognition (ASR) system supports over 30 languages for auto-captioning, including:

Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal), Dutch, Russian, Japanese, Korean, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Hindi, Turkish, Polish, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Finnish, Romanian, Czech, Hungarian, Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Malay, and more.

Auto-caption quality varies by language. Spanish, French, German, and Japanese tend to have very high accuracy. Less-resourced languages may produce more errors.

## Extracting Transcripts from Videos with Multiple Language Tracks

Some videos — particularly from major channels, news organizations, and educational publishers — have multiple manually-created caption tracks in different languages. For example, a TED Talk might have captions in English, Spanish, French, German, and Portuguese.

In these cases, YouTube's transcript panel (on desktop) lets you switch between available languages. Third-party tools vary in how they handle multi-track videos.

## Tips for Non-English Transcripts

**Check auto-caption quality before using.** Open the video in YouTube and check "Show transcript" to preview the captions. If they look heavily mangled, the auto-ASR struggled with this video's audio and any transcript you extract will have significant errors.

**Human-captioned content is more reliable.** Major international channels, news networks, and educational publishers often have human-reviewed captions. These are almost always accurate.

**For language learning, leave timestamps on.** If you're using a foreign language transcript to study, keep the timestamps so you can play back specific sections while reading the text.

**Combine with a dictionary.** Paste the transcript into a web page alongside a dictionary extension like Linguee or WordReference to look up unfamiliar words in context without breaking your reading flow.
