**For video editors, a YouTube transcript turns an hour of scrubbing into a 30-second search.** Paste a published video's URL into [YTTranscript](https://yttranscript.app), get the full transcript with timestamps, and you can find every quotable line, Shorts-worthy hook and recut point by searching text. For your **own raw footage** that isn't on YouTube yet, your editor's built-in transcription (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve Studio or Descript) is the better tool — the two cover different stages of the job.

## Where transcripts fit in an editor's workflow

Most editors already use text-based editing on raw footage. The gap is **everything that's already published**: the client's back catalog, a two-hour livestream you need to cut down, the competitor video you're referencing, last month's podcast that needs ten Shorts. That's where a YouTube transcript tool earns its place.

**Common jobs it speeds up:**

- **Shorts and Reels from long-form.** Search the transcript for hooks ("the biggest mistake…", numbers, strong opinions), note timestamps, cut. See our [YouTube Shorts transcript guide](/youtube-shorts-transcript).
- **Paper edits for recuts.** Paste the transcript into a doc, highlight the lines you want, reorder them, and hand the client a script-style plan before touching the timeline.
- **Titles, thumbnails and chapters.** Pull the exact phrasing for thumbnail text and build chapter markers from timestamps — or use our [chapter generator guide](/youtube-chapter-generator).
- **Client onboarding.** Read a new client's last ten videos in 20 minutes to learn their tone, catchphrases and recurring topics.
- **Captions sanity-check.** Compare YouTube's auto captions with your own SRT to spot misheard names (more on [auto vs manual transcripts](/youtube-auto-generated-vs-manual-transcript)).

## How to do it in 4 steps

1. Copy the YouTube URL of the published video.
2. Paste it into [YTTranscript.app](https://yttranscript.app) and keep **timestamps on**.

## The honest trade-offs

**Your NLE wins for raw footage.** If the clip lives on your drive and hasn't been uploaded, transcribe it in Premiere, Resolve Studio or [Descript](/yttranscript-vs-descript). They let you delete a sentence in the transcript and the cut happens on the timeline — YTTranscript can't do that, and isn't trying to.

**YTTranscript wins when you don't have the file.** Recutting a client's 2023 webinar? Building Shorts from a livestream VOD? Researching a reference video? Downloading the video just to re-transcribe it wastes time and storage. A transcript from the URL takes seconds and works from a phone or a borrowed laptop.

**Limits to know:**

- YTTranscript reads the video's **caption track**. If a video has [no captions](/youtube-video-no-transcript), use Whisper or your editor instead (see [YTTranscript vs Whisper](/yttranscript-vs-whisper)).
- It works on **public** videos. For a client's **unlisted or private** uploads, download captions from YouTube Studio or transcribe the source file.
- Auto captions can mangle names and jargon — fine for finding moments, but proofread anything that goes on screen.

## Verdict: which should a video editor use?

- **Use your NLE's transcription** (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve Studio) for **raw footage you're cutting** — text-based editing is the fastest way to a rough cut.
- **Use Descript** if you mostly edit **podcasts and talking-head content** and like editing video like a document.
- **Use YTTranscript** for **anything already on YouTube**: Shorts from long-form, recuts, paper edits for clients, chapter markers and research. No source file, no account, no install.

Most working editors end up with both: the NLE for the cut, a URL-based transcript tool for everything around it.
