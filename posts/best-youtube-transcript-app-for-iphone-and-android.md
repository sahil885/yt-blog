## 1. YTTranscript: best for most people on any phone

[YTTranscript](https://yttranscript.app) is a website, so it works the same on iPhone, iPad and Android, in any browser. In the YouTube app tap **Share → Copy link**, open yttranscript.app, paste the link and tap **Get Transcript Now**. Because it reads the captions YouTube already generated, even a [3-hour podcast or long livestream](/how-to-transcribe-a-long-youtube-video) comes back in seconds.

- **Free tier:** 1 transcript a day with no account, 2 a day with a free account.
- **Copy and download:** a free account lets you copy the whole transcript or save TXT, DOCX or PDF, with or without timestamps.
- **Heavier use:** one-time credit packs, no subscription.
- **Limit:** it needs captions. For videos without any, see [what to do when a YouTube video has no transcript](/youtube-video-no-transcript).

## 2. YouTube app "Show transcript": best for reading along

The YouTube app has its own transcript. Open the video's description (tap the title on iPhone, or **...more** on Android), scroll to the Transcript section and tap **Show transcript**. Tapping a line jumps to that moment. It's free and needs nothing extra.

The catch is getting the text out. There's no copy-all or download button, and selecting text in the panel is awkward or impossible on most phones. It's perfect for following along; it's not a way to save or share a transcript. Step-by-step help is in our guide to [YouTube transcripts on mobile](/youtube-transcript-on-mobile).

## 3. YouSumm: best native iPhone app for summaries

**Youtube Transcripts – YouSumm** is an iPhone and iPad app that transcribes YouTube videos, writes AI summaries, and exports text, SRT or VTT. It's free to install with in-app purchases for paid Pro tiers, and it holds a **4.5-star rating from about 190 ratings** on the US App Store. Choose it if you want transcripts and summaries inside one native app and don't mind a subscription for regular use. It isn't available for Android.

## 4. QuickScribe Video Transcripts: best for on-device iPhone features

**QuickScribe** (formerly Scribe) turns a YouTube video into a transcript with word-by-word highlighting, on-device AI summaries, and exports to PDF, text, HTML or Markdown. Its release notes say it can transcribe videos without YouTube captions on the device. It's free with in-app purchases, needs **iOS 17 or later**, and has a small number of ratings so far (4.2 from 13). It's iPhone and iPad only.

## 5. NoteGPT: a web option with AI notes

NoteGPT's YouTube transcript generator is a website that works in a mobile browser with no login for the basic generator. Its strength is AI notes and summaries on top of the transcript, which use account quotas and paid plans. On a small screen the page is busier than a single-purpose tool, but it's a reasonable choice if you already use NoteGPT for study notes.

## What about Android apps?

We didn't find a dedicated Android transcript app established enough to recommend. On Android, the practical setup is the YouTube app's transcript panel for reading, plus a mobile website like YTTranscript for copying and downloading. Android's share sheet makes this quick: **Share → Copy link**, then paste into your browser.
