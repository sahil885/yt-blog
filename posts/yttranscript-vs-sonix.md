## When to Use Sonix

Sonix earns its price tag for professional workflows:

- You're transcribing **your own recordings** — interviews, podcast episodes, webinars — not YouTube videos
- You need **speaker diarization** (who said what, timestamped)
- Your team needs **collaborative editing** inside a shared transcript
- You're producing content that requires **99% accuracy** and you'll pay for it
- You need deep integrations with Zoom, Adobe Premiere, or Final Cut Pro

If you're producing original audio content professionally, Sonix is a serious tool. For anything involving a YouTube URL, it's the wrong hammer.

## Pricing Breakdown

**YTTranscript:** Free. Always. No plans, no upgrades, no credit card.

**Sonix:**
- Pay-as-you-go: $10 per hour of audio
- Premium: $5/hour + $22/month per user
- Free trial: 30 minutes (requires sign-up)

For a researcher transcribing 10 hours of YouTube lectures, that's $100 on Sonix — and Sonix can't even pull from YouTube URLs directly. You'd have to download each video first, then upload it. With YTTranscript, those same 10 transcripts take about 5 minutes total and cost nothing.

## Accuracy: Does It Matter Here?

Sonix builds its own AI transcription engine optimised for audio clarity — it claims up to 99% accuracy on clean audio. That matters when you're transcribing a boardroom recording or a podcast episode with background noise.

YTTranscript pulls the captions that YouTube itself generates or that video creators upload. For most professional YouTube content — courses, interviews, explainers — these captions are extremely accurate. Where they fall short is with thick accents, heavy technical jargon, or videos where auto-captions are the only option.

**Bottom line:** If YouTube's captions are good enough (they usually are), YTTranscript gives you the same text instantly and for free. If you need maximum accuracy on your own audio, Sonix has the edge.
