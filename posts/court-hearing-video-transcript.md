**If a court or hearing video is on YouTube, you can turn it into a searchable transcript in seconds:** paste the link into [YTTranscript](https://yttranscript.app) and you get the caption text with timestamps. That transcript is an **unofficial working copy**. It isn't a certified record. For anything you file or formally cite, order the official transcript from the court reporter.

Used for what it's good at, though, the fast version saves a lot of time.

## Which proceedings end up on YouTube

Video of judicial and quasi-judicial proceedings is far more common than it was a decade ago:

- **Appellate oral arguments.** Many state supreme courts and intermediate appellate courts live-stream arguments and keep the recordings on YouTube or their own sites.
- **Administrative and regulatory hearings.** Public utility commissions, zoning boards, licensing boards and agencies often stream them.
- **Public hearings and high-profile trials,** where state rules allow cameras, often appear on news and court channels.
- **Legislative hearings** on legal topics; see our [congressional hearing transcript guide](/congressional-hearing-transcript).

Rules on cameras in courtrooms vary widely by jurisdiction, and most federal trial courts don't allow video, so availability depends on the court.

## Accuracy limits to know

Auto-captions miss **case names, Latin terms, citations and speaker changes**, and caption transcripts don't label who is speaking. Courtroom audio with several microphones makes this worse. Treat every quote as provisional until you've checked it against the video or the certified record. More on legal workflows: [YouTube transcripts for lawyers](/youtube-transcript-for-lawyers).

## FAQ

**Can I get a transcript of a court hearing that's on YouTube?**
Yes. Many state supreme courts, appellate courts and agencies stream hearings to YouTube. Paste the video link into YTTranscript.app to get the caption text with timestamps in seconds, as long as the video has captions.

**Is a transcript from a YouTube video an official court record?**
No. An auto-caption transcript is an unofficial working copy. Official and certified transcripts are prepared by the court reporter or the court's transcription service, and that's what you file or cite in court.

**How do I order a certified court transcript?**
Contact the court reporter who recorded the proceeding, or the clerk's office or transcript coordinator for that court. Pricing and turnaround are usually set by court rule or statute and vary by state and by how fast you need it.

**Are oral argument transcripts available for free?**
Some courts publish their own oral argument transcripts or audio; the US Supreme Court, for example, posts transcripts on its website. Many state courts post only video, which is where a YouTube transcript tool helps.
