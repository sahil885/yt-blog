## Why transcripts matter for agents

Video builds trust, but **text is what gets found**. Google indexes words on your website, and AI assistants like ChatGPT answer "who's a good agent in [town]?" from text they can read. A spoken neighborhood tour is invisible to both until it becomes text.

A transcript also solves the blank-page problem. You already explained the school district, the commute and the new coffee shop on camera — the transcript is your first draft.

Common videos worth transcribing:

- **Listing tours** → listing description, property website copy, Instagram captions
- **Monthly market updates** → email newsletter, local blog post, LinkedIn post
- **Neighborhood and relocation guides** → evergreen SEO pages for "moving to [city]"
- **Buyer and seller explainers** (closing costs, inspections, offers) → FAQ pages and lead magnets
- **Client testimonial videos** → quotes for your website (with the client's permission)

## The 4-step workflow

1. **Grab the transcript.** Paste the YouTube URL into [YTTranscript](https://yttranscript.app). Turn timestamps off for clean prose, or on if you want to link viewers to specific rooms or sections ([how timestamps work](/youtube-transcript-with-timestamps)).
2. **Export it.** Copy to clipboard, or download TXT, DOCX or PDF. DOCX drops straight into Word or [Google Docs](/youtube-transcript-to-google-docs) for your assistant or marketing coordinator.
3. **Repurpose with AI.** Paste the transcript into [ChatGPT](/youtube-transcript-for-chatgpt) or [Claude](/youtube-transcript-with-claude) with a prompt such as: *"Turn this market update into a 600-word blog post for first-time buyers in Austin. Keep my numbers and local references."*
4. **Review and publish.** Check every figure — prices, square footage, days on market — against your MLS data, and read for Fair Housing compliance before anything goes live.

- **Accessibility.** Posting a transcript alongside a video helps viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing and anyone watching on mute.

## A note on compliance

AI tools rewrite fluently but can invent details or use language that steers buyers. Treat AI output as a draft: verify facts against your listing data, avoid descriptions of who a home or neighborhood is "perfect for" based on protected characteristics, and follow your brokerage's advertising rules. The transcript itself is just your own words — the risk is in what you ask AI to add.
