**YTTranscript and Trint solve different problems that look identical from the outside.** YTTranscript is a no-signup web tool that pulls the transcript from a YouTube URL in seconds and exports it as TXT, DOCX, or PDF. Trint is a full transcription and editing platform built for newsrooms, priced at roughly **$80–$100 per seat per month**, that transcribes raw audio and video you upload and gives teams a collaborative editor on top. If your source material is a YouTube link, YTTranscript is almost always the right tool. If your source material is a recorded interview with four people talking over each other, Trint earns its price.

Here's the honest breakdown.

## The core difference: captions vs. speech recognition

Most YouTube videos already have a caption track — either uploaded by the creator or auto-generated by YouTube. A tool like YTTranscript reads that existing track, cleans it up, and hands you a usable document. That's why it's fast and cheap: the hard ASR work was already done.

Trint runs its own speech recognition on whatever file you feed it. That's more expensive computationally, and the pricing reflects it. But it also means Trint works on material that has no captions at all — a Zoom recording, a field interview, a raw camera file.

So the real question isn't "which tool is better." It's **"does my source already have captions?"** If yes, paying newsroom prices to re-transcribe it is money burned. If no, see our guide on [what to do when a YouTube video has no transcript](/youtube-video-no-transcript).

## Where Trint genuinely wins

Give credit where it's due. Trint is a serious product and three things justify its price:

**Multi-speaker audio.** Trint's speaker diarisation is well ahead of what you get from YouTube's auto-captions, which frequently run several people's speech together into one wall of text. If you're transcribing a roundtable or a panel, that matters.

**Team workflows.** Multiple editors working on the same transcript, comments, shared workspaces, permissions. YTTranscript has none of this — it's a single-user utility.

**Editorial output formats.** Trint exports EDL and XML for video editing timelines, and its Story Builder lets you assemble a script directly from transcript highlights. If your transcript is an input to a video edit, that's a real workflow advantage.

**Source material without captions.** This is the big one. Trint doesn't care where the audio came from.

## The verdict

**Choose YTTranscript if** your source is YouTube, you work alone or in a small team, you want transcripts as input for notes, research, or AI tools, and you don't want a per-seat subscription. Free tier plus paid option, no account, no extension.
