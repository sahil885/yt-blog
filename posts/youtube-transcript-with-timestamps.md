### Method 2: YouTube's Built-in Transcript Viewer

YouTube's native transcript panel shows timestamps by default:

1. Open the video on YouTube (desktop only)
2. Click the **three-dot menu** (⋯) below the video title
3. Select **Show transcript**
4. Timestamps appear on the left of each line

You can toggle timestamps off using the three-dot menu inside the transcript panel. To copy, you must manually select the text — there's no download button.

## What Do YouTube Transcript Timestamps Look Like?

A timestamped YouTube transcript looks like this:

```
0:00 Welcome back to the channel. Today we're going to cover
0:05 the three most important things to understand about SEO
0:12 before you start building your website.
0:18 The first thing — and this surprises most people —
0:23 is that Google doesn't rank websites. It ranks pages.
```

Each timestamp represents the moment in the video when that text was spoken. The timestamps are clickable in YouTube's native viewer; in a downloaded transcript they're reference points for manual navigation.

## Why Timestamps Matter

**Citing a specific moment** — If you're quoting something a speaker said, a timestamp lets you link directly to that moment: `https://youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=23s` jumps to the 23-second mark. This is essential for journalism, academic citation, and fact-checking.

**Navigating long videos** — A 2-hour video transcript with timestamps lets you skim to find the section you actually need, rather than scrubbing through the timeline blindly.

**Creating chapter links** — Many creators add chapter markers to videos. If you're building a blog post based on a video, the timestamps help you identify where each chapter begins so you can structure your post to match.

**Content moderation and review** — For legal, compliance, or brand safety review of video content, timestamps create an auditable record of exactly when something was said.

**Podcast show notes** — Timestamped transcripts make it easy to create chapter-based show notes: "12:34 — Discussion of pricing strategy", "28:10 — Q&A begins".

## How to Link to a Specific Timestamp on YouTube

YouTube supports deep-linking to specific moments using the `t` parameter:

```
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=90s
```

This URL opens the video at the 1:30 mark (90 seconds). Replace `90s` with any time value. You can also use minutes and seconds: `t=1m30s`.

To generate this link quickly: right-click on any point in the YouTube video progress bar and select "Copy video URL at current time".
