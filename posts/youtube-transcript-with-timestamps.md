## Why Timestamps Matter

**Citing a specific moment** — Note the timestamp alongside a quote, then link to it: `https://youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=23s` jumps to the 23-second mark. Essential for journalism, academic citation, and fact-checking.

**Navigating long videos** — A 2-hour video transcript with timestamps lets you skim to find the section you need, rather than scrubbing through the timeline blindly.

**Creating chapter links** — Timestamps help you identify where each chapter begins so you can structure a blog post to match the video. See our guide on [turning a YouTube video into a blog post](/youtube-video-to-blog-post).

**Podcast show notes** — Timestamped transcripts make it easy to create chapter-based show notes: "12:34 — Discussion of pricing strategy."

## How to Link to a Specific Timestamp on YouTube

YouTube supports deep-linking to specific moments using the `t` parameter:

```
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=90s
```

This URL opens the video at the 1:30 mark (90 seconds). To generate this link quickly: right-click on any point in the YouTube video progress bar and select "Copy video URL at current time."

Timestamps come directly from YouTube's caption data and are synchronized to the video. They are as accurate as the original captions.
