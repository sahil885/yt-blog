**You can usually get an earnings call transcript free in one of three ways:** the company's investor relations (IR) page, a free publisher such as The Motley Fool, or the call's video replay. Paid platforms are faster and cleaner across hundreds of tickers, but they're priced for professional teams. Here's how to choose.

## Where earnings call transcripts come from

- **Company IR sites** host the webcast, the slides and the press release. Some post a transcript; many only keep the audio replay.
- **Free publishers.** The Motley Fool publishes transcripts for many large US companies. Koyfin, a market-data platform, says its free tier includes 45 days of transcript history.
- **Subscriptions.** Seeking Alpha includes earnings call transcripts with its paid Premium plan, per its help center.
- **Professional platforms.** AlphaSense, Bloomberg, FactSet and Quartr Pro provide fast transcripts with search and alerts, at professional or enterprise pricing.
- **YouTube.** More companies, along with financial media and IR channels, post calls, investor days and conference fireside chats to YouTube. Smaller caps and non-US companies that the free publishers skip often turn up here.

## Accuracy: check the numbers

Auto-captions are good on narrative ("we're seeing softness in China") and weaker on **numbers, tickers and analyst names**. "Fifteen" and "fifty" get swapped, and basis points get mangled. Use the transcript to find the moment, then confirm any figure against the press release, the SEC filing or the replay audio. Caption transcripts also don't label speakers, so the hand-off from CEO to CFO to analyst is inferred from context.

## FAQ

**Where can I get an earnings call transcript for free?**
Check the company's investor relations site first, then free sources such as The Motley Fool, which publishes transcripts for many large US companies. If the call replay is on YouTube, paste the link into YTTranscript.app for a timestamped transcript in seconds.

**Can I get an earnings call transcript from a YouTube video?**
Yes, if the call or its replay is posted on YouTube with captions. YTTranscript pulls the full caption text, which you can search, export as TXT, DOCX or PDF, or paste into ChatGPT or Claude for analysis.

**Are YouTube auto-caption transcripts accurate enough for financial analysis?**
They're good for finding themes and quotes quickly, but they can garble numbers, tickers and names. Check any figure you rely on against the press release, the 10-Q or 10-K, or the replay audio.

**Is Seeking Alpha's transcript library free?**
Not in full. Seeking Alpha's help center says earnings call transcripts are included with Premium, which is a paid subscription. The free plan gives only limited access.

**When are paid platforms like AlphaSense or Bloomberg worth it?**
When you cover many companies and need fast, cleaned transcripts across a whole universe, with search, alerts and history. They're priced for professional teams. For occasional calls, free sources and YouTube transcripts are usually enough.
