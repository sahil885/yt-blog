## 2. YouTube's built-in transcript — best free fallback

Open a video, expand the description, click **Show transcript**. It's free and unlimited, but there's no download button, selecting the text is fiddly, and timestamps come mixed in. Fine for glancing; painful for saving ([how to copy it cleanly](/how-to-copy-youtube-transcript)).

## 3. Gemini Notebook — best for analysis

Google renamed NotebookLM to **Gemini Notebook** in July 2026. Add a YouTube video as a source and ask questions with citations back to the text. It's an analysis tool rather than an exporter — pair it with a transcript you can keep ([our Gemini Notebook guide](/youtube-transcript-for-notebooklm)).

## 4. Tactiq — best if you also transcribe meetings

Tactiq is a Chrome extension built mainly for Google Meet, Zoom and Teams, with a YouTube transcript tool on the side. The free plan gives 10 transcripts a month; Pro is **$8/user/month billed annually** ($12 monthly). Worth it if meetings are your main need ([YTTranscript vs Tactiq](/yttranscript-vs-tactiq)).

## 5. NoteGPT — best for built-in AI notes

NoteGPT bundles transcripts with AI summaries, mind maps and notes. The free plan has 15 basic quotas a month; Pro starts at **$9/month billed annually**, and Unlimited can process 10 YouTube videos at once. Good for students who want everything in one app ([YTTranscript vs NoteGPT](/yttranscript-vs-notegpt)).

## 6. youtube-transcript.io — best for bulk and API

A subscription tool for volume: 25 free transcripts a month, then **Plus at $9.99/month for 1,000** or **Pro at $24.99/month**, with bulk playlist extraction and an API. The right pick if you process hundreds of videos every month ([full comparison](/yttranscript-vs-youtube-transcript-io)).

## 7. Whisper — best for videos with no captions

OpenAI's open-source Whisper transcribes audio itself, so it works when YouTube has no captions. It's free but you install and run it yourself, and long videos take a while ([YTTranscript vs Whisper](/yttranscript-vs-whisper)).
