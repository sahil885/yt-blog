**YTTranscript and Eightify solve two different problems.** YTTranscript gives you the **complete, exportable transcript** of any YouTube video in your browser with no signup and no extension. Eightify is a **Chrome extension and mobile app** that produces **AI summaries and key takeaways** rather than a full verbatim transcript. If you need the actual words, YTTranscript is the better fit; if you only want a quick gist and don't mind installing an extension and subscribing, Eightify is built for that.

Below is an honest, feature-by-feature breakdown so you can pick the right one.

## What each tool actually does

**YTTranscript** is a [YouTube transcript generator](/youtube-transcript-generator) that takes a video URL and returns the full text, with or without timestamps. You can read it, search it, copy it, or export it to TXT, DOCX, or PDF. There's nothing to install, and you don't create an account to use it. It works the same on desktop and [mobile](/youtube-transcript-on-mobile).

**Eightify** is an AI summarizer. Its core value is speed of understanding: install the extension, open a video, and it generates a short summary plus timestamped "key insights" so you can decide whether a long video is worth watching. It also surfaces a transcript, but the product is designed around summaries, not around handing you a clean full transcript to work with elsewhere.

## Pricing, honestly

Eightify uses a freemium model. The free tier caps how many videos you can summarize in a given period, and the useful features live behind a **Premium subscription** that costs roughly **$4.99/month billed annually** or **$9.99/month** if you pay monthly. That's reasonable if you summarize videos constantly, but it is a recurring cost tied to an installed extension.

YTTranscript also has a **free tier plus a paid option** — it is not unlimited and not 100% free. The practical difference is friction: with YTTranscript there's **no account to create and no extension to install**, so you can grab a transcript on a shared or work computer, or on your phone, without setting anything up.

## Which one wins for your use case

**Choose Eightify if** your main goal is deciding whether to watch a long video, and you want a one-click AI summary living right inside YouTube. It's genuinely convenient for that, and the mobile app is handy for on-the-go skimming. This is the case where Eightify honestly wins.

**Choose YTTranscript if** you need the **actual transcript** — to search a specific quote, cite a source, repurpose a video into a blog post, or feed the raw text into an AI model yourself. Because you get the complete text and can export it, it slots into real workflows better. It's the stronger option any time the transcript is the deliverable, not just a summary.

A common pattern: use YTTranscript to pull the full transcript, then paste it into your own model. ## The bottom line

Eightify is a **summary tool**; YTTranscript is a **transcript tool**. If you want to understand a video fast without watching, Eightify does that well. If you want the real words to keep, search, cite, or reuse, YTTranscript is faster to start (no install, no signup) and gives you exportable output. Many people end up using both — Eightify to triage, YTTranscript when they actually need the text.
