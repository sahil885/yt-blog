**The fastest way to get a YouTube transcript on a Mac is to copy the video URL, paste it into [yttranscript.app](https://yttranscript.app) in any browser, and copy or export the text.** It takes about ten seconds, needs no app, no extension, and no signup. YouTube's own "Show transcript" panel also works on macOS and is worth knowing, but it's clumsy to copy from. Here are all three realistic options on a Mac, compared honestly.

## Method 1: Paste the URL Into a Web Tool (Fastest)

1. Open the video in Safari, Chrome, or Arc and copy the URL from the address bar (**⌘ L**, then **⌘ C**).
2. Open [yttranscript.app](https://yttranscript.app) in a new tab.
3. Paste the link (**⌘ V**) and press Enter.
4. Copy the full text, or export it as **TXT**, **DOCX**, or **PDF**.

This works on any Mac, on any macOS version, in any browser — because nothing is installed. It's also the only method that gives you a clean block of prose without fighting timestamps. If you want timestamps kept, see [YouTube transcripts with timestamps](/youtube-transcript-with-timestamps); if you'd rather strip them, see [transcripts without timestamps](/youtube-transcript-without-timestamps).

## Method 2: YouTube's Built-In Transcript Panel

YouTube has a native transcript view and it costs nothing:

1. Below the video, click **…more** to expand the description.
2. Scroll down and click **Show transcript**.
3. The transcript panel opens to the right of the video (or below it on a narrow window).
4. Click the three-dot menu inside the panel to **Toggle timestamps**, then drag-select and press **⌘ C**.

It's fine for reading along or jumping to a moment. It's frustrating for extraction: the panel scrolls independently, long videos require a lot of dragging, and pasted text often arrives with stray timestamps and line breaks that you then clean up by hand.

## Method 3: Install a Mac App or Run Whisper Locally

There are native macOS apps and command-line tools (OpenAI's **Whisper**, plus various App Store transcript apps) that download the audio and transcribe it on your machine. This is the heavyweight option.

It's genuinely the right choice in two cases: when a video has **no caption track at all**, and when you're processing **dozens of videos in batch** and want it done offline. Otherwise it's a lot of setup — installs, models, and processing time measured in minutes rather than seconds — for text you could have had immediately. ## The Verdict

**Use a web tool** if you want the text and want it now — research, quotes, study notes, meeting-adjacent videos, or [pasting a transcript into ChatGPT](/youtube-transcript-for-chatgpt) or [Claude](/youtube-transcript-with-claude). This covers the large majority of Mac users.

**Use YouTube's built-in panel** if you only want to skim or jump to a timestamp and don't need to keep the text.

**Install Whisper or a native app** if the video has no captions, if you need offline processing, or if you're batching many videos. It's the most capable option and the slowest — that trade is worth making only when you actually need it.

## FAQ

**How do I get a YouTube transcript on a Mac?**
Copy the video URL from the address bar, open yttranscript.app in Safari or Chrome, and paste it. The full transcript appears in seconds and can be copied or exported as TXT, DOCX, or PDF. No app or extension is needed.

**Does YouTube have a built-in transcript on Mac?**
Yes. Under the video description, click the three-dot menu and choose "Show transcript". The panel opens beside the video. It works, but the text is timestamped line-by-line and awkward to select and copy cleanly.

**Do I need to install a Mac app to download a YouTube transcript?**
No. Any browser-based tool works entirely in Safari, Chrome, or Arc. A Mac app is only worth installing if you process large volumes of videos and want offline batch handling.

**How do I save a YouTube transcript as a PDF or Word file on Mac?**
YTTranscript exports directly to TXT, DOCX, and PDF. Alternatively, paste the text into Pages or TextEdit and use File → Export as PDF.

**Why does a video have no transcript on my Mac?**
The problem isn't your Mac. Some videos have captions disabled by the creator, or are private or restricted, so there is no caption track to read. In that case you need audio transcription instead.
