## Method 2: YouTube's Built-in Transcript Feature

YouTube has a native transcript viewer you can access without any third-party tool.

1. Open the video on YouTube (desktop browser only)
2. Click the **three-dot menu** (⋯) below the video title
3. Select **Show transcript**
4. A panel opens on the right with the full text and timestamps

The limitation: there's no download button. You have to manually select all the text and copy-paste it somewhere. On mobile, this feature doesn't exist at all. For a quick glance it works fine; for anything more, a dedicated YouTube to text converter is much faster.

## Method 3: YouTube's Caption API (For Developers)

YouTube exposes caption data via a timedtext endpoint:

```
https://video.google.com/timedtext?lang=en&v=VIDEO_ID
```

Replace `VIDEO_ID` with the 11-character ID from the video URL. This returns raw XML with timestamps and text segments. Useful for developers building applications, but not practical for everyday use.

## What Affects Transcript Quality?

When you convert a YouTube video to text, the quality of the output depends on the source caption data:

**Human captions** — Created by the video uploader or a professional transcription service. Usually 99%+ accurate. Used by channels like TED, Khan Academy, and major news networks.

**Auto-generated captions** — Created by YouTube's speech recognition AI. Typically 85–95% accurate for clear English audio. Can struggle with heavy accents, technical jargon, or background noise.

**No captions available** — A small number of videos (very old content, poor audio quality, or manually disabled captions) have no transcript data to extract. In this case, you'd need a dedicated audio transcription service like Whisper or Rev.

For most videos you'll encounter, auto-generated captions produce perfectly usable text. Just double-check any technical terms or proper nouns.
