**To get a recipe from a YouTube video, first check the description and pinned comment for a written recipe or blog link; if there isn't one, copy the video's transcript and ask an AI chatbot to turn it into an ingredients list and numbered steps.** That second method works on almost any cooking video with captions and takes about a minute. Recipe-saving apps can automate it, but they cost money once you go past a few recipes.

Here's each method, what it's good for, and where it fails.

## Method 1: Check the description and pinned comment (free, 10 seconds)

Many cooking channels put the full recipe — or a link to it on their website — in the **video description** or a **pinned comment**. On mobile, tap the title to expand the description. If a link is there, that's the creator's own tested recipe with exact measurements. Use it.

The catch: lots of creators, especially on Shorts, don't write anything down, or link to a site buried in ads and pop-ups. That's where the next method comes in.

## Method 2: Transcript + AI chatbot (free, about 1 minute)

Most of what you need is in what the cook **says out loud**: ingredients, rough amounts, temperatures, timings. A transcript captures all of it.

1. **Copy the video link** from YouTube (Share → Copy link).
2. **Paste it into [YTTranscript](https://yttranscript.app)** and copy the transcript. Turn timestamps off for cleaner text — see [transcripts without timestamps](/youtube-transcript-without-timestamps).
3. **Paste it into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini** with this prompt:

> "Turn this cooking video transcript into a recipe. Give: title, servings, an ingredients list with quantities, and numbered steps with times and temperatures. If a quantity isn't stated, write 'not specified' instead of guessing."

That last line matters. **If the cook never says an amount aloud, the AI may invent one** unless you tell it not to. Fill any gaps by glancing at the video.

## Method 3: A recipe-saving app (paid for regular use)

Apps like **ReciMe**, **Honeydew** and **Pestle** can import from a YouTube link and file the result into a cookbook with shopping lists and meal planning. If you save several recipes a week, that organisation is worth paying for. Their free tiers are deliberately small.

## FAQ

**How do I get a written recipe from a YouTube video?**
First check the video description and pinned comment for a written recipe or blog link. If there isn't one, copy the video's transcript with a tool like YTTranscript and paste it into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini with a prompt asking for an ingredients list and numbered steps.

**Can ChatGPT turn a YouTube cooking video into a recipe?**
Yes, if you give it the transcript. Paste the transcript and ask for ingredients with quantities and step-by-step instructions. Check the quantities against the video, because if the cook never says an amount aloud the AI has to guess.

**What is the best app to save recipes from YouTube?**
Recipe apps such as ReciMe, Honeydew and Pestle can import from YouTube links and keep recipes in a cookbook with shopping lists. Their free tiers are limited, so they suit people who save recipes often; for an occasional recipe, a transcript plus an AI chatbot costs nothing.

**Is there a free way to convert a YouTube recipe video to text?**
Yes. YTTranscript's free tier gives 2 transcripts a day with no signup, and the free tiers of ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini can turn that transcript into a formatted recipe.

**Why are the ingredient amounts missing from my YouTube recipe transcript?**
A transcript only contains what the cook says out loud. Amounts shown as on-screen text or visible on measuring cups won't appear, so check the description, the creator's blog, or the video itself for those numbers.
