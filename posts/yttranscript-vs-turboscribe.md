One instantly pulls the captions YouTube already has; the other re-transcribes audio from scratch with AI. Picking the right one comes down to what you are transcribing and how much friction you will tolerate. Here is an honest, side-by-side breakdown.

## The Core Difference: Captions vs AI Transcription

This is the single most important thing to understand. **YTTranscript** reads the caption track that YouTube has already generated for a video and hands it to you as clean text in seconds. **TurboScribe** ignores any existing captions and runs the audio through **Whisper**, OpenAI's speech-to-text model, producing a fresh transcript.

That difference explains everything else. Pulling existing captions is instant and free — but it works where captions exist, which for YouTube is almost everywhere. AI transcription works on *any* audio, including files that have no captions at all, but it takes processing time and, on TurboScribe's free tier, counts against a daily limit.

## TurboScribe: AI Transcription for Any File

TurboScribe is a general-purpose AI transcriber. You upload an audio or video file (or paste a YouTube link), and it generates a transcript using Whisper, with optional speaker recognition and subtitle exports.

**Strengths:**
- **Works on any file** — MP3, MP4, interviews, podcasts, meetings, not just YouTube
- **Speaker recognition** — labels who is talking, useful for multi-person audio
- **Subtitle formats** — exports SRT and VTT for captioning your own videos
- **Strong on raw audio** — Whisper handles messy recordings and accents well
- **Many languages** — supports 98+ spoken languages for transcription

**Limitations:**
- **Account required** — you sign in, and your files live in a dashboard
- **Free tier is capped** — 3 transcripts per day, 30 minutes each
- **Slower** — every transcript is processed from scratch, taking minutes
- **Paid for real volume** — unlimited use is $10–20 per month
