You don't need expensive software or hours of typing to **transcribe a YouTube video**. If the video has captions — and almost all do — you can get an accurate text transcription in seconds, for free. This guide covers exactly how, how accurate it is, and how it compares to paid transcription services.

## Two ways to "transcribe" a YouTube video

There are really two situations:

**The video has captions** (the vast majority). YouTube already has the text — either typed by the creator or auto-generated by speech recognition. A free tool extracts and formats it instantly. This is what most people need. Learn more about the [difference between auto-generated and manual captions](/youtube-auto-generated-vs-manual-transcript).

**The video has no captions at all** (rare). There's no text to extract, so you'd need a paid speech-to-text service that processes the raw audio. For most YouTube content, you'll never hit this case.

## How accurate is it?

Accuracy depends on the captions behind the video. **Manual captions** (typed by the creator) are near-perfect. **Auto-generated captions** are usually very good for clear audio but can stumble on background noise, strong accents, or technical jargon. Either way you get a complete draft in seconds that you can quickly clean up — far faster than transcribing from scratch. Need exact moments?
