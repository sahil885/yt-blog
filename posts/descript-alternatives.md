**The best Descript alternative depends on one question: are you editing video, or do you just need the words?** Descript is a video editor that happens to transcribe — if you only wanted the text, you are paying editor prices for a transcript. For YouTube URLs, a no-signup tool like **YTTranscript** gets you there in seconds. For uploaded audio files, **Otter** or **TurboScribe** cost far less. For broadcast-grade accuracy, **Rev** still wins. Descript itself remains the right answer only if you genuinely edit video by editing text.

Descript's 2026 pricing is a free plan, then **Hobbyist at $16/user/month billed annually ($24 monthly)**, **Creator at $24/user/month annually ($35 monthly)** and **Business at $50/user/month annually ($65 monthly)**. Each tier caps transcription hours, and AI credit top-ups can push the real bill above the sticker price. This guide compares the seven alternatives worth considering and says plainly who each one is for.

## The real question: transcript or timeline?

Descript's defining feature is that deleting a word in the transcript deletes it in the video. That is genuinely useful, and nothing on this list replicates it as cleanly. But it is also why Descript is priced like an editing suite rather than a transcription tool.

If your actual job is *reading, quoting, searching or summarising* what was said, you do not need a timeline at all. That is the entire reason the cheaper tools on this list exist, and it is the honest reason most people searching for Descript alternatives end up switching.

## Best for uploaded files on a budget: TurboScribe and Otter

**TurboScribe** is the closest thing to a straight price cut from Descript for file uploads: **$10/month billed annually ($20 monthly)** removes the daily cap, and its free plan allows 3 transcripts per day at 30 minutes each. It does not edit video, which is the point.

**Otter.ai** is the better pick if your recordings are live meetings. Its free tier gives **300 minutes per month** with a 30-minute cap per conversation — but note the catch: only **3 lifetime file imports** on free, so it is built for recording meetings, not uploading a backlog. ## Best for accuracy: Rev and Whisper

**Rev** is where Descript loses cleanly. Human transcription at roughly **$1.99 per minute** is the standard for depositions, medical notes and anything that will be read by a court or a client. Its AI tier at about **$0.25 per minute** is cheaper but not meaningfully better than the free options here.

**Whisper** is the other honest win against Descript. It is free, open-source and runs on your own hardware with no per-minute meter and no files leaving your machine — which matters for confidential recordings. The trade-off is setup time, GPU cost and no interface. Our [YTTranscript vs Whisper comparison](/yttranscript-vs-whisper) covers when the extra work pays off.

## Best for subtitles and translation: Happy Scribe and Sonix

If your output is a caption file rather than a document, **Happy Scribe** (about **$0.20/minute pay-as-you-go**, with subscriptions from roughly **$8.50/month billed annually**) and **Sonix** (usage-priced per audio hour, roughly **$5–$10/hour** depending on plan) both handle subtitle formats and translation better than Descript's export options. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free Descript alternative?**
It depends on your source. For YouTube videos, YTTranscript is the fastest free option because it needs no account and no extension — you paste a URL and get text. For uploaded audio files, Otter's free tier (300 minutes per month) is more generous, and self-hosted Whisper is free with no cap at all.

**How much does Descript cost in 2026?**
Descript has a free plan, then Hobbyist at $16/user/month billed annually ($24 monthly), Creator at $24/user/month annually ($35 monthly), and Business at $50/user/month annually ($65 monthly). Transcription hours are capped per plan, and AI credits can add overage costs.

**Why do people look for Descript alternatives?**
Three reasons dominate: price for solo users, the transcription-hour caps and AI credit top-ups, and the fact that Descript is a full video editor when many people only wanted the transcript text.

**Is there a Descript alternative that doesn't require an account?**
Yes. YTTranscript pulls transcripts from a YouTube URL with no signup and no browser extension. Descript, Otter, Rev, Sonix, TurboScribe and Happy Scribe all require an account before you can transcribe anything.

**What is the most accurate Descript alternative?**
For difficult audio, Rev's human transcription (about $1.99 per minute) is the most accurate option, and Whisper large-v3 is the strongest automated one. For YouTube videos that already have captions, accuracy gaps between tools are small because they all read the same caption track.
