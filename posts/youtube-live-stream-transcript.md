## The one rule: the stream must have ended

While a stream is live, YouTube can show **live automatic captions**, but Google's help page says these are **available in English only**, and they aren't supported on ultra-low-latency streams such as many mobile live streams. More importantly, they **don't stay on the video**: after the stream ends, YouTube generates new automatic captions for the replay through its normal processing, and they may differ from what viewers saw live.

So the full transcript exists only once the replay has been processed. Google doesn't publish a timeline; it says processing time depends on how complex the audio is. In practice, short streams are usually ready sooner than multi-hour marathons.

If you try to extract a transcript and get nothing, the replay hasn't been captioned yet, so **wait and retry**. If days pass with no captions, the creator may have turned them off or the language may not be supported; see [what to do when a YouTube video has no transcript](/youtube-video-no-transcript).

## Accuracy notes for live content

Replay captions are [auto-generated](/youtube-auto-generated-vs-manual-transcript), and live content is harder to caption than scripted video: multiple speakers, crosstalk, audience noise, variable microphones. Expect good results on clear single-speaker audio and noticeably worse ones in chaotic moments. Names, brands, and technical jargon are the usual casualties — worth a quick check before quoting anything verbatim.

For a cleaner read, copy the [transcript without timestamps](/youtube-transcript-without-timestamps); for quoting, keep them.

One more quirk: auto-captions on replays generally don't label speakers. If you need a speaker-by-speaker record of a panel, use the timestamps to identify who's talking at each point and annotate the downloaded file.

## Premieres, restreams, and other special cases

**Premieres** are uploaded in advance, so they're captioned like regular uploads rather than live streams. **Restreamed or re-uploaded streams** get fresh auto-captions like any new upload. **Unlisted replays** work like public videos if you have the link; **members-only and private replays** generally can't be read by outside tools, so use YouTube's own transcript panel while signed in.
